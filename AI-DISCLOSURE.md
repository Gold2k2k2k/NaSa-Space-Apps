# AI assistance and generated data

The team used AI coding assistance, including Antigravity in its earlier workflow
and Codex in this continuation, for code analysis, implementation, tests, design
discussion, documentation and presentation drafting. These tools assisted the
work; their output is not scientific authority. Contributors remain responsible
for checking the code, attribution, claims and final submission. Exact historical
model/tool versions were not independently established and are not invented here.

The running application uses deterministic original Python and browser code; it
does not contact an AI model. Named pipeline roles are software stages, not
independent human researchers or external model agents.

Generated regional presets, custom-geometry demo records and synthetic sample
CSVs are reproducible fixtures and labeled accordingly. They cannot substantiate
real regional trends, impacts or prediction accuracy. Satellite opportunities
are approximate modeled passes without orbital ephemeris validation.

The Central Highlands case contains actual NASA FIRMS archive records. Its
values were not invented or filled by AI. The reproducible builder clips a
declared rectangle, preserves original fields, sorts rows and hashes all sources.
Confidence filtering, clustering, descriptive reference statistics and fitted
models are transformations with documented limits. Missing observations remain
unknown; no complete-coverage product or field validation is supplied.

No actual land-manager interviews/usability study, causal fire attribution,
operational warning validation or measured management impact has been completed.
The submission narrative reports negative FRP agreement and distinguishes software
verification, sensor consistency and independent physical validation.
